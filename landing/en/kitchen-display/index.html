A printer in the kitchen? The wood age.

Bluetooth and network ticket printers have done their job for twenty years. Kitchens got faster since; paper did not. Here is an honest comparison.

In the kitchen Ticket printer (Bluetooth / IP) Hugo Kitchen Display
Connection

Bluetooth drops through a wall; a network printer wants a cable, a fixed IP address and a router nobody dares to touch.

Wi-Fi and a browser. The display pairs with a code; no IP addresses, no cables.

Order

Tickets on a rail, on the counter, on the floor. Which one is oldest only the longest-serving cook knows.

Sorted by time. You see how old each order is at a glance, by colour.

Priority

The waiter shouts “this one’s urgent!” over the extractor fan.

A priority order jumps to the top and lights up.

Notes and allergies

Small print at the end of the ticket, smudged by greasy fingers.

Large and highlighted right next to the dish they belong to.

Alerts

The quiet whirr of a printer nobody in the kitchen can hear.

A sound for every new order. You set the volume.

Order changes

A new ticket prints. The old one still hangs there; guess which one counts.

The order updates in place. No cooking it twice.

When the paper runs out

Friday, 8 pm. Orders vanish into thin air until someone notices.

It can’t run out. There is none.

Kids’ portion

5 km of thermal paper a year

That is how much paper one kitchen printer pushes out.

Picture a walk across Prague’s old town and back. Every metre was paid for, torn off, pinned to a rail and thrown away. And thermal paper is not recyclable with ordinary paper, so it ends up in general waste.

Estimate: 100 tickets a day, 15 cm per ticket, 330 opening days a year.

What paper can’t give you.

The display is not just a ticket on a screen. It is an overview of the whole kitchen.

  • Sound

    A new order chimes. You hear it over a sizzling pan.

  • Ordering

    The oldest order always comes first. No digging through tickets.

  • Priority

    An urgent order jumps to the top and is visible from across the kitchen.

  • Notes

    “No onion”, “allergy: nuts”, “kids’ portion”. In large type, by the right dish.

  • Timer

    Every order shows how long it has waited. Green, yellow, red. You know where the fire is.

  • Done in one tap

    The cook taps “Done” and the floor knows it can serve. No running, no shouting.

That old tablet in the drawer? It just got a new job.

As long as it isn’t from the stone age: an iPad from 2017 or newer (iPadOS 16.4+), an Android 10+ tablet (roughly 2020 onwards) with an up-to-date Chrome, or any computer with a current browser. That is what it takes for the display to chime and stay awake.

What you’re asking

How much does the kitchen display cost?

We are opening it to the first venues in early access. Write to us and we’ll set you up to try it in your own kitchen.

What do I need?

The Hugo POS and a device with an up-to-date browser and an internet connection: an iPad from 2017 or newer (iPadOS 16.4+), an Android 10+ tablet or a computer. For a tablet we recommend a wall mount or a stand, so it stays out of the sauce.

What if the internet goes down?

The display needs a connection, just like a cloud POS. No order gets lost, though: once the connection is back, the display fetches the ones that came in meanwhile.

Can I have a display and a printer?

Tell us how your venue is split and we’ll suggest a setup that fits. A display in the kitchen and tickets at the bar is a common combination.
